Dört Akademik Pasaj — Okuma Uygulaması

Ders · Tür dizini

Her pasaj dört soruluk grupta ilk soruda verilir. Sonraki üç soruda aynı pasajı kullan. Çalışmalar öğretim amacıyla kurgulanmış özgün senaryolardır.

Cevaplarını, güven düzeyini ve kanıtını ayrı kâğıda yaz. Yanıt anahtarını bitirince aç.

Soru 1

Pasaj 1 — Environment / Economics

A coastal city offered grants to small businesses that installed water-saving equipment. During the first year, water use fell in participating shops, while it changed little in shops that did not join. Officials initially treated this difference as evidence that the equipment alone had caused the reduction. However, participating owners also received regular advice on maintenance and were unusually willing to monitor their bills. The evaluation could not separate the effects of the equipment from those of advice and monitoring. Moreover, participation was voluntary, so the two groups may have differed before the programme began. The evaluators did not argue that the grants were useless. Instead, they recommended a future trial in which otherwise similar shops would receive different combinations of equipment and advice. Such a design could provide a clearer account of which parts of the programme were responsible for the savings.

What is the main point of the passage?

  • A) The observed savings cannot be attributed solely to equipment without a better comparison.
  • B) Water-saving equipment had no effect in any shop.
  • C) Voluntary programmes always produce reliable comparisons.
  • D) Advice was proven to be the only cause of savings.
  • E) Grants should be removed because all participants wasted water.

Soru 5

Pasaj 2 — History / Archaeology

For decades, historians dated the abandonment of a hillside settlement by the newest pottery found there. A recent excavation uncovered a repaired storage chamber beneath a later layer of soil. The chamber contained seeds dated to a period after the supposed abandonment. At first, this seemed to show that the settlement had remained continuously occupied. Yet a storage chamber need not imply the presence of a permanent community. Travellers might have used an empty settlement as a temporary place to store supplies. To distinguish between continuous occupation and occasional reuse, the team began comparing signs of everyday activity in different parts of the site. Repeated repairs to houses would support the former interpretation; isolated objects without household activity might support the latter. The new evidence therefore changes the question more than it settles it: the team now asks how the site was used rather than simply when it was abandoned.

What is the author mainly doing?

  • A) Arguing that all storage chambers belong to permanent communities.
  • B) Explaining why new evidence leaves two interpretations open.
  • C) Proving that no traveller ever visited the settlement.
  • D) Defending pottery as the only valid dating method.
  • E) Announcing the exact date of permanent abandonment.

Soru 9

Pasaj 3 — Psychology / Education

In a classroom study, students learned unfamiliar concepts through either repeated reading or short retrieval tasks. Both groups spent the same amount of time with the material. Students who reread reported greater confidence immediately afterwards, but those who practised retrieval performed better on a test one week later. The researchers suggested that the feeling of familiarity produced by rereading can be mistaken for durable knowledge. This does not mean that reading is unnecessary. Students must first encounter and understand information before trying to retrieve it. Nor does the result imply that every retrieval task is useful: questions that merely invite guessing may reveal little about understanding. The researchers recommended a sequence of initial reading, explanation in one’s own words, retrieval and corrective feedback. They also noted that the study involved only one age group, so further work would be needed before applying the results to all learners.

Which contrast is central?

  • A) Adults versus children in different countries.
  • B) Correct feedback versus the complete absence of any test.
  • C) Immediate confidence versus delayed test performance.
  • D) Longer study time versus shorter study time.
  • E) Reading before retrieval versus no exposure to information.

Soru 13

Pasaj 4 — Medicine / Technology

A regional clinic introduced a digital tool that flagged patients who might need an earlier follow-up appointment. The tool did not diagnose illness; it combined information already available in medical records. Staff found that it helped them review long patient lists more quickly. Nevertheless, speed was not their only concern. Some records contained missing or outdated information, which could affect the tool’s recommendations. The clinic therefore required a professional to check each recommendation before contacting a patient. This safeguard slowed the process slightly but reduced the risk of acting on incomplete information. The developers proposed adding more data sources, whereas the clinicians first wanted clearer explanations of how each recommendation had been produced. Their disagreement concerned the best way to improve trust, not whether the tool should replace professional judgement. The clinic planned to evaluate both accuracy and workload before extending the system to other departments.

The tool was designed primarily to ___ .

  • A) replace all medical records with new measurements
  • B) contact patients without any review
  • C) prove that missing data never affect decisions
  • D) flag patients for possible earlier follow-up
  • E) diagnose every illness without staff involvement
